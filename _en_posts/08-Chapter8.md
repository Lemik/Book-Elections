### Volunteers and agitators

It is known that volunteer assistants save time, effort and resources for the election campaign. Although, ultimately, their work costs the candidate more than the work of hired professionals.

Volunteer helpers

In any public activity, and especially when conducting election campaign, it is impossible to do without the involvement of assistants.

these are enthusiasts. They do the work that the Assistants do not have enough time, effort, or money to do. It is also an excellent talent resource that is of great value to election campaign.

even-

In the implementation of voter contact programs, the main thing is to set tasks for agitators (volunteers) and monitor execution. It is quite difficult to draw the line between the work of an agitator and a volunteer. Both the responsibilities and forms of work of both are very similar. The main difference is that the agitator receives a specific reward for his work.

Agitators, as a rule, are connected with the candidate by personal obligations: family, official, party, material or friendly. Therefore, they are easier to organize and easier to control.

Voluntary helpers (volunteers)

These are activists from among voters, driven by other, non-material motives: attracted by ideological considerations or sympathy for the candidate himself (his program), or a desire to solve a number of social issues. Quite often they have a specific personal interest or request for the candidate, timed “after the victory.”

The importance of the work of agitators and volunteers depends on the level of the election campaign. It increases significantly if elections are held at local levels of government (district, city) and the possibility of using the media is limited. In this case, only through the use of direct campaigning can one create conditions for “live” communication and establish direct contact with the voter. To reduce the costs of campaigning, you can also use a common network of campaigners with candidates for other levels of government (for the regional council and the Verkhovna Rada). But before agreeing to such cooperation, try to assess how such a tandem will affect the image of your candidate: whether he will play for him or against him.

Features of work

A door-to-door campaign is the most effective way to convey information about a candidate and his program to voters. But it requires the participation of a large number of employees. Therefore, one of the important tasks of the election campaign is to create a core of very strong and dedicated volunteers and agitators. The more volunteers you can involve, the more significant financial resources you will have

save money.


However, it should be noted that a paid staff of agitators works much more productively and professionally, is easier to manage, and can be counted on to a greater extent. The work of agitators and volunteers is often combined, but they should not be allowed to do the same job together. If you use paid workers and volunteers at the same time, do not assign them to the same task. Negotiate the amount of payment and distribute responsibility so that there are no misunderstandings.

It is best if a separate room with a telephone is used for working with volunteers (not on the headquarters premises). This will not only allow us to receive more people, but also protect the work of the headquarters from prying eyes and “infiltrators.”

How to attract volunteers

To implement the election campaign program, a special system for attracting volunteers should be built. The basis of this system is the ability to ask for help. Don't hope that someone will come and offer it. It is easier for a person to agree to provide a service than to offer it. To work with volunteers, there must be a separate manager who searches and attracts volunteers from everywhere, coordinates their activities and maintains appropriate records.

He must make sure that these are sufficiently trained people, clearly assign a task to each of them and appreciate their work. At the same time, it is important to remember: you are not attracting people under your wing, but inviting them to join the political process. Show them what they can achieve by helping you and how specifically they can help with your CI.Let them know that they are vital to you and that you appreciate their efforts.

First you need to figure out why people agree to work unselfishly. For a person new to politics, it may seem surprising when someone devotes their time, energy, and abilities to work in the election campaign.

How to work with volunteers

You should not rely solely on well-known, already proven, but perhaps tired volunteers from political activity. There are always people who are not experienced in political struggle. They may be new to big politics, but well-known and respected people in their community.

Contact future volunteers and be clear about exactly what kind of help you expect from them and when. Volunteer work should not be left to chance, otherwise it becomes unmanageable and does more harm than good.

You should keep basic records - a card index where you can enter all the data about the volunteers and their capabilities, what kind of assistance the election campaign can provide.

Responsibilities of an agitator

## The responsibilities of the agitator include:

collecting signatures in support of a candidate;

conducting a primary survey (about the needs of the area, attitude towards the elections, support for the candidate);

- drawing up a map of the area: number of houses, doors, planning campaign routes, identifying suitable places for posting posters;

- distribution of information materials at meetings with voters;

conducting campaign conversations: making contact, presenting the candidate and his program, listening (registering) questions and wishes, removing objections, leaving contact;

- formation of lists of unambiguous supporters with voters’ telephone numbers (see “Election Day”);

report to the foreman;

maintaining a route map;

putting up posters in the area;

recording the actions of rivals and rumors wandering around;

dissemination of measures to suppress rumors, obtaining necessary information, launching counter-rumours.

Appoint foremen

A prerequisite is that the foreman must be able not only to successfully contact voters, but also to organize and structure the activities of agitators. Depending on the complexity of the areas, 5-12 agitators are under his command. The powers and responsibilities of foremen include:

placement of agitators in areas and setting specific tasks;

preparing weekly assignments for each agitator and volunteer;

- providing agitators with the necessary information and propaganda materials and report forms;

establishing a system for monitoring the activities of the agitator, systematizing the results of campaign campaigns and reports;- preparation of lists of supporters and volunteers;

preparing reports on the progress of the campaign, evaluating the work of agitators and presenting them “for awards.”

Training of agitators

Hold a meeting between the candidate and campaigners. Remember, they should become your most devoted and conscientious assistants. Get to know them better, tell them about yourself, fill them with optimism and confidence in victory.

The head of the territories must conduct a detailed briefing with them to explain the goals and objectives of the election campaign, the main provisions of the program and the biography of the candidate. If possible, try to prepare agitators in a group. This is not only simpler, but also allows you to work out the most standard contact scenarios in a game situation and develop the most acceptable contact style for each agitator.

It is not enough to show how this is done; it is important that the agitator himself finds his own, the most environmentally friendly and natural way of contact. Only then will his agitation be sincere and convincing and will not keep him in constant tension.

Particular attention must be paid to working through critical situations and developing optimal contact strategies for all agitators. This could be a negative reaction (“it’s none of your business,” “I can’t stand your candidate,” “screw you...”) or unexpected tricky questions.

Construction of questions

There are closed questions (to which a monosyllabic answer is possible: “yes” or “no”), open questions, paraphrasing questions, distracting questions, and suggestion questions.


Questions allow you to activate the attention of your interlocutor, involve you in a conversation, establish feedback, and obtain information about your interlocutor, his opinions, attitudes, and preferences. When formulating a question, you need to know exactly what answer/reaction you want to receive. You should ask those questions that persuade the interlocutor to agree with you (the “yes, yes, yes” method).

The ideal question is one that the interlocutor wants to answer. Collect good questions.

Rules of conduct for agitators

Western textbooks on conducting election campaigns usually give very specific and clear advice, which in our conditions often requires adjustments. For example, the agitator must know whose doorbell he is ringing, he must step back to “lure” the voter out of his apartment onto his territory, he must never enter the house, the conversation must last no more than 30 seconds, etc.

Even if the campaigner has a complete list of voters, it is hardly worth addressing the voter by name; the likelihood that he will like it, that someone outsider knows his name, is too small. Our people are accustomed to the fact that anonymity is safer. The fact that someone unfamiliar and alien has personal information carries a threat.

Many people, even when participating in completely non-political and completely harmless surveys, first of all find out whether their participation will be anonymous. Or, when conducting telephone surveys, they begin to be biased in finding out where the interviewer got their phone number, and do not believe in the randomness of the sample.

If the door is still opened, it is indeed better for the agitator to step back, demonstrating that he does not intend to enter



into the house, and give the voter the opportunity to leave his territory. But if the agitator is invited to enter, he should not refuse. The reasons for such an invitation can be different: from a simple runny nose to the opinion that a conversation on the threshold is a manifestation of complete bad manners. A conversation on neutral territory is more advantageous in the sense that, firstly, the voter is more easily influenced, and, secondly, less time is spent on opening and closing doors and on communication itself. But, on the other hand, by refusing an invitation to enter, you can offend a person and thereby lose his voice.

As for the duration of the conversation itself, it is unlikely that you will be able to keep it exactly. Of course, it is better if you can provide the voter with campaign materials, answer his questions about the candidate, and at the same time not get caught up in conversations about some extraneous things. On the other hand, a person often starts a conversation with a stranger about what is painful, and by listening to him, the agitator can thereby win him over, and at the same time, towards the candidate. People often perceive the fact that “now there is no one to even complain to” as the latest degree of “lawlessness”.

In addition, who, if not the agitator, can collect live information about the problems of voters or recruit active supporters. Canvassers can also create lists of those who would like to be informed about upcoming meetings with the candidate and those who are unable to come to the polling station due to health reasons but want to vote for your candidate.

Under no circumstances should you argue with voters. The agitator's task is not to create discussions, but to provide information about the candidate and make a good impression. ### Time

The best time for an agitator is when people are at home. This is evening time on weekdays and midday on Saturday and Sunday. Don't be afraid to come at dinner time. This may be the only opportunity to catch the voter at home. Keep visits short but cordial. Apologize if you interrupt someone's dinner. It is best to carry out work no later than 8-9 pm. The hosts are friendlier, less anxious, and the hikers feel more confident.

Appearance

Your clothing should make a good impression on the audience you meet. Don't wear expensive clothes or a tie. No matter what you wear, you should look neat and uncluttered. Remember, the candidate is judged by the campaigner.

How to control the work of an agitator

The issue of monitoring the work of agitators is quite sensitive. It’s good if the agitators are proven, reliable people. Otherwise, the control problem will have to be solved.

Canvassers should be aware that a certain percentage of their work will be subject to scrutiny (eg 10-15%). It is unlikely that a good way would be to simply go to several apartments that have already been “passed” by agitators and inquire about their work. It will look like a waste of time by people who have nothing better to do.

The solution could be, for example, this trick: the inspector works under the guise of an agitator and, in response to the remark: “They’ve already come to us,” he responds with distress: “They probably mixed up the houses,” simultaneously finding out how those who already came acted.

## Campaigning must be carried out quickly and efficiently. To calculate the production rates for one agitator and the number of agitators required to implement the contact program, it is necessary to know the average contact time in a given area. The average contact time is determined empirically for each specific campaign area and depends on the following circumstances:

duration of contact. They are distinguished: successful contact, lasting 3-7 minutes, contact-failure - 0.5-1 minute, contact - “sticking” 15-20 minutes;

- time of transition to each door (it will be different for apartment buildings and the private sector);

percentage of “empty” apartments at a given time in a given area. Multiplying the average contact time by the number of doors in an area will give the time required to conduct a door-to-door contact program. As a rule, a fairly limited time is allocated for the contact program - two to three weeks. Thus, the number of canvassers required will depend on the time required to conduct the program divided by the canvassing time.

How to encourage the work of agitators

The work of an agitator is difficult and must be paid or rewarded (non-financial incentives).

There are two types of payment for the work of agitators: piecework (for each contact or for each successful contact) and time-based.

Piece payments encourage the agitator to make more contacts, but may also encourage him to overcharge or reduce the quality of his work. Therefore, this form of payment is used along with a comprehensive campaign quality control system.

A special incentive system may be in place for attracting volunteers.

The costs of conducting a “every door” campaign usually top the list of expense items in the election campaign budget. Therefore, this area requires special attention and control from the head of the election campaign.
