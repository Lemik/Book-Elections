# Chapter One

## Election campaign

One of the Kyiv journalists noted that if the last elections in Russia were held under the slogan: “Vote, or you lose,” then the next elections in Ukraine will be held under the slogan: “Vote, or your neighbor wins!”

### What is an election campaign?

From the many existing definitions of an election campaign, we chose the one that best suits our style.

---

*An election campaign (EC) is a set of targeted, consistent efforts carried out over a period of time specified by law in order to mobilize voter support for a candidate and ensure his victory on election day.*

---

Therefore, all efforts in a campaign are aimed at making the best use of available resources to enhance the candidate's advantages and neutralize the strengths of his opponents. During the election campaign process the following occurs:
* forming a group of supporters and stimulating their activity on election day;
* attracting to your side people who will vote, but have not yet decided for whom;

* weakening the positions of opponents and introducing doubts and disagreements into the camp of their supporters (counter-propaganda). The main character of the election campaign is a deputy candidate for a specific elective position. The nature of election campaign involves the joint work of a group of people (teams) who, together with the candidate, overcome the path to the set goal. Candidates who have already lost elections know that it is impossible to win the election campaign on their own, without outside help. The more people are interested in a candidate's victory, the more likely that victory is.

Each election campaign is not like the others. The uniqueness of the campaign is determined by three components:
1) the personality of the candidate: his abilities, experience, education, views, character and temperament, as well as his connections and obligations;
2) the specifics of the moment - geographical and demographic features of the district, the degree of urbanization, the presence and specificity of the media, the number of volunteer assistants, funding opportunities, etc.;

3) election campaign strategy: political goals, theme, focus, schedule, budget, tactics.

## Factors for a successful campaign

Studying the experience of successful political campaigns, we can note five main factors that determine the technology of an election campaign:
1) through analytical work, the needs, goals and real capabilities of individual priority groups of voters are assessed;
2) election campaign activities are systematically planned and implemented in a timely manner;
3) the results of the work done are constantly monitored and analyzed, tactical changes are made to plans;

4) special importance is attached to the role of interpersonal communication and methods of conveying information to the voter;

5) the candidate occupies a central place in the entire election campaign, but there is a clear functional structure, and the main “dirty” work is performed by assistants and a support group.

Types of election campaign


The form and content of election campaign depend on many factors. In this book we use only some types of election campaign.

1. Depending on the level of representative office being filled and on the size of the electoral districts (number of residents), we distinguish the following scales of an election campaign:

- A local small-scale election campaign: elections of deputies to village or district councils (up to 5,000 voters).

- A local mid-scale election campaign (5,000–20,000 voters in the district).

- A local large-scale election campaign (20,000–50,000 voters); a regional campaign (over 50,000 voters); a mid-scale regional campaign: elections to the Verkhovna Rada and mayoral elections in medium-sized cities (over 100,000 voters).

- A large-scale regional campaign: mayoral elections in large cities (over 200,000 voters).

- A nationwide election campaign (the entire population of Ukraine).

The smaller the scale, the simpler the campaign structure and the more emphasis shifts from media to the candidate’s personal contact with voters. The larger the scale, the less the candidate is involved in day-to-day local issues, and the greater the role of the headquarters and the demands on the team’s professionalism. This book does not cover strategy and methods for a presidential campaign.

2. Depending on how elections are combined—that is, whether a given round is for one level or several at once, or in other words whether the voter receives one ballot or several on election day. Combined elections often cover several levels of government (district, city, regional councils, and the Verkhovna Rada) or pair an election with a referendum.

3. Depending on the type of electoral system, elections are held: according to a majoritarian system, each electoral district has one mandate, and all candidates compete for the right to receive this mandate;

according to the proportional system

lists of candidates are nominated for a multi-member territorial constituency. Mandates are then distributed in proportion to the number of votes received;

according to the mixed system

Some of the mandates are distributed among single-member districts, and some among multi-member districts in the proportion established by law.

Let us leave outside the scope of our consideration the existing combinations and variants of electoral systems. The specifics of elections are determined by the current legislative framework, and therefore previous elections are not similar to the next.

We also do not consider all possible options for the electoral barrier, the features of early and repeat elections, the advantages of positive (“for” - mark) or negative (“against” - cross out) voting. Let us only note that such a change in the “rules of the game” has a very serious impact on the voting results and complicates the ability to rely on the results of previous elections when analyzing and forecasting.

It should be said that elections in rural constituencies have their own specifics: the political culture and traditions of voters, the presence of local media, the peculiarities of campaigning and the elections themselves - all this is sharply different from the peculiarities of conducting election campaigns in urban conditions. We do not consider the specifics of election campaign in rural districts in this book. But, since almost 75% of the population of Ukraine lives in cities and towns, this book will be useful to all specialists in conducting election campaign.

Let us only note that the doubling of majoritarian electoral districts to the Verkhovna Rada of Ukraine creates serious problems for candidates in rural districts; in addition, they need to maintain two types of election campaign - for the rural population itself and for the cities of the region.

Election campaign model

There are many election campaign models. A model is a methodological basis, a set of generalizations and terminology on which the organizational group (team) of the election campaign is based.

The book describes the election campaign marketing model, which is actively used by advertising firms, which often undertake to “lead” election campaign.

In such a model, the voter acts as a hypothetical Buyer, and all kinds of electoral associations (individual teams, parties, political blocs) act as hypothetical Sellers. The role of the Product is assigned to candidates, their programs, image, etc.


Thus, such an election campaign model can be defined as the process of creating a Product, developing, planning and carrying out activities to identify the Product, advertise its advantages and stimulate sales. And the election campaign’s goal is to hold a huge (district-wide) one-day sale with the greatest effect.

The modern market is impossible without active and even aggressive political marketing. It involves studying the conditions of the political market, the mood of voters and their willingness to support certain programs.

Based on socio-psychological research of the district, the campaign strategy, its focus, means and methods of implementation are outlined. At the same time, all election campaign actions have a clear focus on individual (target) categories of voters, and the organization of the campaign is as structured and rational as possible.

election campaign factors

In election campaigns, it is customary to consider two types of factors:

controlled, which depend on the decisions and actions of the candidate himself and his team and can be monitored using special socio-psychological research (surveys, public opinion monitoring, focus groups); uncontrollable. These are phenomena of social reality that objectively influence the course of election campaign. They are not subject to influence and do not depend on the actions of the applicant and his headquarters. You should never lose sight of uncontrollable factors and try to incorporate them into your strategy in such a way that they play in the candidate’s favor or are at least perceived neutrally.


Uncontrollable factors include:

the general vector of socio-political processes in the country; actions of central, regional and regional authorities; economic trends in the country as a whole and in a particular district; features of the environmental situation;

features of the social portrait of the region;

the nature of the activities of opponents and their teams;

a sharp change in the political situation in neighboring countries (for example, the death of a political leader or a coup);

weather conditions, etc.

Voter categories

"core".

Every candidate has natural supporters. These are people who you know in advance that they will vote for your candidate for a number of reasons. They can be considered won by voters, provided that the election campaign strategy is aimed at strengthening and expanding this “core”.

Within the “core” we can identify a very promising group of voters - opinion leaders. These are those who enjoy trust and authority in their circles and therefore shape the political culture and preferences in their circles. Most often, these are acquaintances (neighbors, co-workers, famous journalists or cultural figures) who “understand politics.”

It is important to promptly identify such people in your district and create privileged conditions for connections (primarily informational) with them.


There is also the largest category of voters who are undecided in their preference and attitude towards voting, the so-called “swamp”. The task of the election campaign is to stir up this “swamp” through targeted activities (the “every door” contact program, visiting enterprises, organizing rallies).

And finally, critical or hostile categories of voters are identified - “lost” votes. The election campaign's strategy is to neutralize this category of voters by shaking their confidence and reducing negative prejudice against your candidate. For this purpose, debates with rivals, counter-propaganda, etc. are used.

It is important to determine the candidate’s position in relation to each of these categories: “we don’t see it point-blank,” “we’re trying to interest you,” “we’re actively courting,” “we’re warming up sympathy for us,” “we’re asking for specific help and support,” etc.

Of course, such an approach should not damage the integrity of the candidate’s image and the content of the election campaign.

Types of Motivation

Studying the reasons that push (or, conversely, hold back) a person to participate in political events in general, and in the electoral process in particular, one can notice that they are quite easily recognized and classified. This makes the process of working with different groups of voters definite, making it possible to describe the lines of their political activity, a set of standard models of behavior and the main ways of effectively influencing each of the groups. Depending on the main motive that determines a person’s participation in the electoral process, the following types of electoral motivation can be distinguished.

1. Positive motivation “why am I going to the polls”: vague responsibility: “I must vote”, “If not me, then who?”;

positive activity: “I want x to win”; negative activity: “I don’t want X to win”; passive elections: “I don’t care - everyone votes, and I vote”;

ideological motivation: “I vote for an idea (party), and not for a specific person”;

inertial voting according to tradition, habit: “I have always voted and now I will vote”; voting “like everyone else”: “Everyone is going, and I’m going.”

2. Negative motivation “why don’t I go to the polls”: vague indifference: “Why do I need this?”; negative passivity: “Anyway, my vote doesn’t decide anything...”;

protest: “This doesn’t concern me”;

absence of a hero: “There is no one to choose...”.

It is very important to recognize a specific behavioral type in time and be able to effectively influence it (especially in the work of an agitator).

Preference factors

Whether a particular candidate is chosen or not is ultimately decided by the preference factors that influence the voter's decision making. These factors are of an emotional nature and decisively determine the outcome of the elections. Here are some of them:

degree of familiarity with the candidate. An argument like: “I know him well. I've heard a lot about him" is often decisive. Most people do not specifically search for information about who is running as a candidate in their constituency.

These people live their daily lives. They, as a rule, do not strive to attend a rally at any cost and do not try to scrupulously understand the advantages and nuances of political programs. Most often they are friendly but skeptical. And, if some information captures their ears, it is often in spite of, rather than thanks to, their attention.

This is why the “information wave” is so important. All other things being equal, preference is given to the one who is more well-known, who is more humanly close and understandable;

conscious attitude towards the candidate and his program. People don't always share the candidate's point of view. But even in this case, a consistent, frank and confident position is very impressive and respectful. Remember, strength is respected;

emotional acceptance (liking/disliking) of the candidate; recommendations of “opinion leaders” often make the final decision (not always consciously) under the influence of recommendations from authoritative, significant persons, relatives, and acquaintances.

#### Publicity of trust

As a rule, the opinion of a person who understands politics and is trusted can be decisive. A person happily gets rid of responsibility and the need to make decisions on his own.

Election campaign goals

Depending on the stage of election campaign, these goals may be different. For example, at the stage of getting to know a candidate, all materials should work to promote his image. Six main objectives of election campaign can be described.

1. Name identification.

You can't win by being nothing. The voter must know the name of the candidate even before his image is created, problems begin to be discussed, or rivals begin to attack. Therefore, famous (even scandalous) people have a significant advantage over unknown newcomers.

Most election campaign to local councils is based on name identification. To quickly remember the name of your candidate, there are many ways: from “high-profile” cases and rumors to “landfall advertising” (flyers, badges, posters, photographs).

2. Creating an image.

The main goal is to make it easier for as many residents of your district as possible to identify with the candidate. We accept a point of view much easier and more willingly if the interlocutor is similar to us, similar to us. Then we approve of his standards of behavior and adapt to his assessments. Image opens up the possibility of emotional acceptance of the candidate (it is useless to ask why he is liked “like him because I like him”). The majority of voters (especially the female part) belong to the emotional type of voters.

3. Statement of problems.

Many people vote based solely on the issues the candidate addresses and the proposed solutions to them (the rational type of voter). At the same time, it is important to concentrate on a few subjects and not strive for all-inclusiveness.

Debates with opponents, speeches at rallies and in the media are the best ways to demonstrate your candidate's positions. Be sure to make sure that the issues being raised are vital to your constituents and that the proposed solution is feasible.

4. Use of contrast.

It is very important to differentiate yourself from competitors. It is important that your candidate's views contrast with those of his opponents. This helps to crystallize the candidate’s image and distinguish him from the general crowd.

It is important that comparisons with competitors are always based on facts. The voter does not admit unfounded accusations. Your attacks should always be supported by evidence. An effective attack can be carried out using many methods. The more supporting documents and facts, the more successful the attack. Printed information that is placed in mailboxes, press publications, and television revelations are better suited for this purpose.

You can get rid of troubles if you act through third parties who will attack your opponents. At the same time, the third party must be credible and have authority.

5. Protection.

You must have a program for “responding” to criticism of your candidate. Before responding to your opponents' attacks, try to assess the degree of danger and possible consequences. It often happens that an attack provokes demoralization and diversion of the main forces from strategic actions.

6. Call to vote.

It is necessary to create a situation so that people who are close to your views and support you come to the polling stations and vote for the candidate. Radio and television broadcasts with respected members of the community endorsing your candidate are effective at this stage.

This goal is most successfully achieved if the situation is presented as critical, and the vote of your supporter can decide your victory. Ultimately, the main goal of election campaign is to create a mechanism for forming public opinion. In other words, the candidate must be seen by as many people as possible, in his best light, and under the most favorable circumstances. It is also desirable that as a result of your election campaign, the voter can answer the question for himself: “why am I voting?”and “who exactly should I vote for?”

Propaganda and agitation

The origin of the term “propaganda” dates back to the 16th century, when the Congregatio propaganda fide was created by Pope Clement VII.

During the Great French Revolution, this concept acquired a political meaning, as a designation of a type of political activity to disseminate a certain ideology, in contrast to agitation (agitation) a call for specific actions and actions: for example, voting for or against a candidate or not voting at all.

Modern propaganda has two features: mass, pervasiveness and impersonality, standardization. Propaganda splits the usual social structures (family, cultural communities, religious groups), isolating each of us and at the same time driving us into a silent crowd - a crowd of loners. Such a crowd is more easily susceptible to propaganda hoaxes, be it the hoax of “developed socialism,” “perestroika,” or “market economy.”

Preparation of propaganda


Preparing voters to accept propaganda is a strategic action used to build the foundations of a candidate's future image. It begins long before the elections, and its activity is of a background nature. Preparation is based on the use of several methods:

1) provoke a “collective consciousness” among certain categories and groups of voters. One way of such deindividuation is the assertion that voters in a given category have common interests and common opponents and that only by uniting they represent strength;

2) create support groups based on unions and associations that meet local and regional interests. Experienced politicians usually create (or contribute to the creation) of such local groups to “protect the interests” of any community in the constituency, such as associations of farmers, commodity producers, retired officers, defrauded investors, etc.;

3) change the image of the candidate and disseminate images and ideas, which will then become the starting points of propaganda during the election campaign. Particular attention is paid to dispelling prejudice against the candidate;

4) “catching simpletons,” which in the jargon of propaganda specialists means the opportunity to attract a prominent personality (an artist, scientist, doctor, astronaut) to one’s side, ask him to speak out on some general or private non-political issue, find common ground and thereby “bind” this personality, penetrate his circle.

election campaign financing

Financing the election campaign is one of the problems that parliamentary candidates face long before the start of the election marathon. Conducting a high-quality election campaign in modern conditions is very expensive - expenses can range from the minimum specified by law to the equivalent of $1 per voter.

It is no coincidence that the main part of political marketing is the search and collection of funds for election campaign fundraising, as such work is called in foreign technologies.

Sources of funding

Funds going to candidates' election funds are divided into:

funds allocated by the election commission are equal for all candidates;

the candidate's own funds;

- funds of an electoral association or political party;- voluntary donations from individuals and legal entities

persons

Legal requirements

The maximum amount of a candidate's expenses from the entire election fund may be limited by law.

Restrictions may be imposed on participation in the formation of funds for certain categories of investors (for example, foreign countries cannot participate in financing).

When forming an election fund, carefully study the legislation and relevant instructions and analyze (preferably with a lawyer and accountant) the following points: - is there a limit on the amount of the election fund; Are there any restrictions on accepting donations from certain categories of investors, what are these restrictions;

how the expenditure of election fund funds is regulated;

- who has the right to manage the funds of the fund;

what are the requirements for keeping records of receipts and expenditures of funds;

what is the procedure for opening a special bank account;

when the candidate must provide a financial report on the use of funds from the fund;

what documents need to be provided;

what you need to be careful about when spending funds from the fund.

Collecting money from the public

This is the most difficult and painstaking method of fundraising. For us, he is unusual and not popular, although abroad there is a tradition of collecting money from the population, and the amount of funds raised is considered as a kind of criterion for the popularity of a candidate. If enough money is not collected from voters, this is an objective indicator of the candidate's future failure.

In our conditions this is of little use. Money can only be collected from those who have it. When collecting money from the population, you should clearly assess the appropriateness of this event in each case, possible public outcry, comments in the media, and your opponents’ interpretation of the fact of collecting funds from voters.

Therefore, fundraising from the population is the most intimate and unpredictable part of election campaign.

Collecting money from business circles

The business community refers not only to entrepreneurs, but also to a whole cohort of joint-stock companies, state-owned enterprises and various “influence groups.”A business person, even if he is a close friend of the candidate, will not give money just like that “for beautiful eyes.”Therefore, you should not give up when you fail and persistently strive for contacts, even in the absence of reciprocal enthusiasm. It is also a mistake to assume that absolutely all entrepreneurs are selfish in their pre-election assistance. The truth, as always, lies somewhere in between these two extremes.

During the negotiation process, the interests of both parties should be taken into account. Always remember that you are asking for money not for yourself personally, but for the campaign. And if legality and a sense of proportion are observed, then the obligations that the candidate undertakes are absolutely moral and correct.

Foreign election campaign technologies widely use such forms of fundraising as holding banquets for influential and wealthy campaign sponsors, sending personal letters (several times during the election campaign) asking for support at a decisive moment in the political struggle. Potential donors must keep abreast of events at all times: they are regularly sent newspaper clippings, leaflets, programs, and the results of sociological surveys.

Fundraising from public associations

When raising funds, it is necessary to remember the existence of various types of public organizations: trade unions, cultural and sports associations, non-political associations, youth clubs, etc.

Typical mistakes when working with them:

the opinion that “they themselves have no money”;

the idea that assistance can only be expressed in monetary terms. The law requires the use of election funds only, but does not prohibit the use of volunteer assistance in implementing contact programs. And this is money, and sometimes a lot of it;


opinion about their apoliticality. Although there is a tendency towards a decrease in the overall activity of the masses, any organized structure, with the appropriate approach, is quite easily politicized.

How to collect money

Fundraising is a procedure where a candidate or a member of his team acts as a petitioner. As a rule, no one gives money voluntarily; the owners of the funds must be asked, convinced and explained.

1. Formation of fundraising should begin with at least a rough estimate of the expected expenses (see “Campaign Planning”), so that the question: “How much and for what purposes do you need funds?”the potential “donor” could receive a direct and honest answer.

2. The strictest accounting is necessary, even in small things. On the one hand, this is direct savings, but on the other hand, even a trifle can ruin a campaign.

3. It is very appropriate to be careful in choosing a depositor and in the forms of receiving a deposit. Don't give your opponents the opportunity to compromise your candidate through careless actions.

4. Candidates who already hold a high position have greater credibility and advantages when it comes to receiving funds (especially at an early stage of election campaign).

5. There is a general rule for any fundraising, called abroad: “Even the postman rings twice!”Money will be brought into the election fund only by numerous and regular consultations and meetings with potential sponsors and donors.

6. If you are refused financial assistance, do not rush to proudly leave the office. Good questions like: “Well, okay, you have problems with money. How could you help our campaign? We would be satisfied with the following” and a long list of necessary things.

With some persistence and planning, resources can be replenished anywhere. However, the candidate must always remember that by accepting donations, he takes on certain obligations that may subsequently affect his capabilities. Therefore, it is worth carefully weighing how much to take and from whom to take it. Remember that deposits can only be accepted into the election fund (after opening an appropriate bank account). Otherwise, the candidate seriously risks his reputation.
