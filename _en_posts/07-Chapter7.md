Competitor analysis

### Competitors

## a necessary attribute of recent generations of elections. When working with them, the following general principle is taken as a basis: “It is better to overestimate your opponents a little than to underestimate them.”To work successfully with competitors you must:

1) identify general (main) competitors - by analyzing rivals (see “Initial characteristics”). There may be many rivals, but only two or three pose a real danger;

2) track the dynamics of the “fame rating”. The list of general competitors can change quite quickly, so you have to constantly keep them in the field of attention. The conclusion about the rating of rivals is usually made based on the results of surveys and expert assessments;

3) draw up a special plan of protective and neutralizing measures within the framework of current legislation.

What is the minimum set of knowledge about competitors? Below is a small list of necessary information.

1. What are they?(Everything that can be learned about them, staying within reason.)

2. Their strengths.

3. Their weaknesses.





4. Their possible social base.

5. Who is on their campaign team?

6. Proposed campaign strategy.

7. Supposed techniques to be used.

10. Image in the media and its dynamics.

12. Degree of voters’ satisfaction with them (why do they like them and why do they not like them?).

Types of opponents

## All potential rivals can be divided in relation to issues of principle (issues of ownership, political orientation, ideology) into the following groups:

“fellow travelers,” when rivals are people with similar views;

“polar explorers,” or clear opponents, whose views are diametrically opposed and have no fundamental points of convergence;

- “gray horses”, with an uncertain attitude and image. It’s easier to “fight” bright, polar opponents. Therefore, for strong opponents, several “fellow travelers” are put up, who “take away” the votes of supporters of a particular idea.

District selection

There are certain rules for choosing the district in which your candidate is going to run.



Ideally, there should be only one candidate in the district with the same views and program as your candidate. In this case, only he will get the support of voters. If there is someone else like him, the votes will be divided between the two of them. Thus, the success or failure of an election campaign depends on the number of people with similar agendas coming forward in one constituency.

It is better to have several opponents with diametrically opposed programs than a “twin brother”; it is easier to build an election campaign.

Political forces of extreme orientations (communists, ultra-nationalists) act very coherently, fielding only one candidate in each district. As a rule, there are several candidates with democratic views of different shades in the district, and they dilute and scatter the votes of democratically minded voters, thereby ensuring victory for more organized rivals.

Therefore, it is advisable for candidates with similar views to agree on which of them will “make the game” and which should give in as less promising. Then a “similar” competitor can objectively weigh its chances and determine the terms of the “concession.”

In reality, this happens extremely rarely, since the candidates’ personal ambitions, as well as a history of interpersonal conflicts that have nothing to do with politics, interfere.

Therefore, it is extremely important to “separate” candidates with similar views into different constituencies. This is the task of regional political elites and various associations (alliances) of candidates.

Voter groups


Different groups of voters have different values, which depend on the size of the group, its sphere of influence, the degree of organization and the presence of a management structure, the existence of an authoritative leader capable of influencing the opinion of the group, the homogeneity of the group, and sources of information.

A coalition is a collection of different groups of voters united by independent interests that can provide support and vote for your candidate in an organized manner.

Tracking competitors' actions


A very important area of the headquarters’ work is foreseeing and neutralizing the actions of rivals. Avoid “surprises” from your opponents, try to anticipate their initiatives, actions and attacks. To do this, the headquarters must carry out work (intelligence) to monitor the activities of rivals and plan and carry out countermeasures.

Negative campaign by competitors

During a negative campaign that your rivals may launch against you, three types of information are produced and disseminated.

The first includes the so-called compromising evidence - true information that the candidate, however, would like to hide, because he suspects that it will be negatively perceived by voters and will spoil his political image.

The second type includes discredit or slander.

And the third is the presentation of this or that information about the opponent, which he considers positive, in a negative light. For example, to a candidate’s assertion that he is wealthy and, having managed to earn money for himself, will be able to work effectively in the economic and budgetary areas, competitors may respond that his wealth makes him far from

How to react to your opponents' actions

As a rule, in the last days of the election campaign, various incriminating evidence is extracted from dusty boxes, which can include both real “dark” spots in the candidate’s biography, and simply the most incredible and unimaginable fabrications. The candidate must be ready to learn a lot of new and unusual things about himself.

Many of these attacks do not cause any particular harm to the election campaign, but simply provoke the candidate and his team into rash actions, with the goal of either diverting forces “to fight rumors” or demoralizing your candidate and causing panic in your ranks. These days he needs a cool mind and your support. Be prepared for this turn of events.

Don't show your weak points. Develop a “crisis” protection plan and be prepared to put it into action. If you can afford it, provide additional opportunities for a prompt response to your opponent’s attack or refutation.

In addition, you can use the strategy of “vaccinating” the press: anticipating an attack from your opponent, be the first to touch on a sensitive topic. This is very risky, but it will help you seize the initiative and gain certain advantages.

### Rumors

## Rumors are the most powerful channel for forming public opinion. They can arise spontaneously, being a reaction of group or individual consciousness to an event that has two main characteristics:

1) it is significant for the subject or group;

2) there is a lack of official information on it. Therefore, it is not always the case that campaigners and other team members bring various rumors about a candidate from the district, this is the work of competitors. It is possible that the candidate's political behavior causes people to feel uncertainty and anxiety, and in order to reduce circulating rumors, he simply needs to provide the population with more information about himself. In this way, it is possible to combat naturally occurring rumors that implement the function of self-informing society.

## As for not the spontaneous emergence of rumors, but their deliberate launch, we can point to a number of purposes for which they are spread:

discrediting an opponent in the eyes of the population. So, for example, in the last elections, a series of rumors were launched against one of the most popular candidates in their district by a rival’s team, which worked quite effectively, since they were conceived taking into account the characteristics of the electorate of this district.

For example, in the area of ​​the departmental buildings of one of the factories, a rumor was spread that this candidate had once worked at this factory as a foreman, was rude and insulted the workers.

dates

In the area where, shortly before the election campaign, a number of commercial kiosks were built, which caused great irritation among residents, a rumor was started that this candidate was the owner of these kiosks, etc., provoking the population to commit actions beneficial to one of the conflicting parties. We are talking about mass riots, strikes, excessive demand for food, etc. Rumors about the complete disappearance of products, spread by the opposition, lead to people actually buying up all goods, causing prices to rise and shortages. Residents place the blame for the current situation on the government in power;

misleading the opponent by understating or exaggerating one’s own strength and capabilities (“500 agitators work for me”).

through the media, most often, messages generating rumors are submitted with the remark “we can report based on unverified information”, “our correspondent learned from unofficial sources”, “based on information received from trustworthy persons”, etc.;- in conversations at work (the carriers and spreaders of rumors through this channel are more often men);

Counterattack technique

Working with opponents is especially important at the end of the election marathon. Most often, the strongest and most unexpected “trump cards” must be held until the last minute and only used if you are sure that you will achieve the desired effect. Such attacks are unlikely to attract additional supporters to your side, but they can shake their confidence and discourage swing votes from your opponent.

Negative attack material can also force your opponent to answer a question that he doesn't want to address on election campaign.
