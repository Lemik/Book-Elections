### Initial characteristics

## The chapter tells how to start getting acquainted with the district. This is information that no candidate ever fully possesses, but everyone should have. Here we will talk a little about the ways and methods of collecting, searching and analyzing such information.

Initial characteristics of the district

Modern elections cannot be conducted without having a well-defined set of high-quality, complete, reliable and up-to-date information. The information resources of the campaign, along with financial ones, are the most important link in further sound analysis and development of the strategy that should lead to victory. Information used in election campaign is divided into information obtained as a result of various types of research, and information obtained from existing sources. The collection of information begins from the first, initial stages and ends after the announcement of the election results.

composition of the electorate;

results of previous elections;

- by competitors (comparative analysis).



This is a collection of information that comes from various sources: statistical, demographic, economic data, results of public opinion research, studies of alleged opponents, and much more.

The ability to quickly access existing information about a region is very important. Many data on issues that interest you exist in the form of a data bank of local councils, statistical offices, property funds, tax inspectorates, various public utilities, etc. It is clear that each of the listed structures formally keeps this data deeply secret, but practical experience shows that with sufficient persistence and systematicity, such data can be obtained.

Based on the collected information, a complete and high-quality analysis is made on all positions of interest to the team. Defined:

1) the real state of affairs in all areas of interest to you: what is happening?where does it happen?how is it happening?and perhaps why?

2) planned trends in the development of processes: what is moving, where, how quickly and why?

3) possibilities for managing processes, their options and management conditions: where, why, how, at what cost, due to what and for how long?

All this is described in detailed reports. Subsequently, the strategy of the entire campaign is built on their basis.

Local candidates have a certain advantage over “outsiders” from local fame and knowledge of the district’s problems to the confidence that “houses and walls help.”But even if you were born and have lived all your life in this district, be sure to conduct such an analysis and put your conclusions on paper.

Economic information about the district

First of all, it is necessary to collect comprehensive data relating to industry, entrepreneurship and the local budget. You need to know the number of enterprises of all forms of ownership located in the district and their qualitative composition, i.e.how many and what kind of enterprises, plants, factories, institutions, commercial structures, service enterprises, etc. are in the district. In addition: the quantitative composition of working voters by enterprise (meaning those who live and vote in your district - they can work anywhere);

- average wages by enterprise and income levels of voters, both basic and additional (with a list of possible sources);

- financial condition of the main enterprises (having the largest share), profitability, loss ratio, debts (structure of debts to whom, for what?), non-payments, etc.; a list of the main problems of enterprises at this time;- structure of the district budget: who are the main payers of taxes to the local budget and what are the main items of expenditure (preferably with real figures);

employment levels and sources of possible unemployment; supply of the district with products, goods and services, average prices for basic products, price dynamics; a list of the main and “painful” (i.e., unsolved for a long time) problems of the district, possible ways to solve them;

economic features of this district (expert estimates); Characteristics and structure of property (especially housing, land, non-residential buildings and production).

Mapping the County



The first task that you should set for the leaders of groups of agitators (foremen) is to get to know your campaign area, scan the area and collect information about the number of its inhabitants. To do this, the foreman must go to a specific polling station and draw a street layout, outlines of houses, note the number of entrances, floors and the total number of apartments in each specific building.

He includes in a separate list the institutions located in this building (libraries, shops, dormitories, etc.), with a brief description. He also marks public transport stops on the map. In the future, when the foremen will place agitators in polling stations and determine daily tasks, this first acquaintance with the area will be very useful to them. Each foreman should know his campaign area in person, be easy to navigate and be aware of all its problems and features.

The location of the polling station is also plotted on the map, and approaches to it are studied. A separate list includes telephone numbers, surnames, first names and patronymics of site employees.

In order to combine all the disparate diagrams into a single map, you will most likely need the help of a specialist cartographer. However, all this work will greatly facilitate further practical work with the region.

plan various events in the best way, coordinate the work of agitators and volunteers;- choose the most convenient places for meeting with voters and posting leaflets and posters;

- identify the most problematic areas and priority areas for concentration of election campaign efforts.

Election research

## In modern political election campaign, all actions of the candidate, his staff and the team as a whole must be based on competent and extensive election research. For election campaign, the most relevant are the so-called basic studies. These include the following:

constituency and voter research (public opinion);- media and communications research; research of opposing candidates; self-research for the candidate and the team.

Research methods

1) public opinion research (conducting surveys);2) analysis of the results of previous elections;

3) univariate demographic analysis;

4) multivariate cluster analysis;

5) interviewing representative groups;

6) monitoring of public opinion;

7) analysis of the most important problems of the region;8) analysis of the opponent’s campaign.

A detailed description of each of the listed methods and a dozen others, more complex and simpler, is in the specialized literature, and their strengths and weaknesses are well known to sociologists, analysts, statisticians, etc.

Any of these methods, individually and in all possible combinations, can and should be used during election campaign. The only question is the competence of those who set the research task, the professionalism of the researchers, the availability of sufficient material resources for the customer, and the availability of time resources for the performers, plus everything about the fact that the customer has a sufficient choice of performers.


As practice shows, carrying out all the research work yourself is a waste of time and effort. Compiling questionnaires, forming a sample, creating a network of questionnaires, processing and analyzing data to master all these types of work, the election campaign is not the best time. Therefore, if you do not know how to do this, it is better to contact specialists.

Public Opinion Research

Public opinion research is carried out in two main forms:

1) quantitative research involving a survey of a statistically representative sample of voters;

2) analysis of focus groups.


A sociological survey of voters is an initial, quantitative survey of the electorate. The survey makes it possible to establish the most important points regarding the characteristics of the district, the candidate and his rivals, to find out the points from which one can build when developing an election campaign strategy.

Such a county survey may include identifying the following:

1) electoral intentions;

2) attitudes towards the position of the district in the city and in the region (whether its development is going in the right direction);

3) assessment of the economic situation (whether the economy is strengthening or becoming weaker, whether the life of a particular individual is improving or worsening);

4) rating of the problems most acutely facing various groups of voters;

5) strengths and weaknesses of participants in political struggle;6) the candidate who is the most credible and leading in terms of his approach to key political and economic issues.

Such research will provide a complete map of the political territory in which the campaign will unfold. They will help to identify the weaknesses and strengths of parties and candidates, the concerns and fears of the electorate, as well as areas favorable for the election campaign, and those where your position is weaker.



However, they will not allow us to penetrate into the area of ​​​​the deeper fears, hopes and value orientations of the electorate. A different type of research is needed here - focus groups, or in-depth psychological studies of motives and beliefs.

Focus groups

When conducting focus groups in an experimental situation, groups of voters consisting of 6-8 people and directed by a specially trained facilitator are studied. Interviewing them does not produce statistically reliable results, but it does provide insight into the mood of the electorate. It is impossible to do without this when conducting a modern election campaign.

## Analysis of focus groups allows, in particular, to determine:

what really causes fears and concerns among voters; what they really hope and fear; what they really think about politicians, whether they like them or trust them;

what their beliefs and deepest values ​​actually are; what kind of response does political information evoke, how do posters, slogans and leaflets influence them;

on the basis of which the voter decides to vote for one candidate or another.

Focus group analysis provides depth, while public surveys provide quantitative indicators. Your election campaign will undoubtedly need both.

Voter segmentation

Each candidate, each electoral bloc or party, going to the elections, must determine in advance their social base, which is their support. This is done by identifying conditional groups of voters based on a common criterion. Segmentation can be carried out:

- on the range of key issues common to the voter;

according to socio-demographic principles (gender, age, social status, standard of living, etc.); by place of localization (residence);

in relation to political and economic issues (property, political system, state language); on professional grounds, etc.

From the point of view of an ideal election campaign, it is necessary to develop such a number of campaign substrategies that correspond to



corresponds to the number of groups of voters in your district. In practice, this is impossible and impractical; however, it is necessary to identify very specific groups of voters and use their commonality for effective and targeted work with them.

The choice of target group is determined by a number of the following reasons: - development of targeted and more personal, specific, and therefore more effective political slogans and appeals; efficient use of available resources;- easy implementation of local (territorial) features. The main time should be for priority groups, and additional time for everyone else.

Examples of voter groups: workers, engineers, employees, rural residents, veterans, urban entrepreneurs, rural entrepreneurs, teachers, health workers, military personnel, students, pensioners, youth, refugees, etc.etc.

Target groups

targeted and quite a lot

In order to create an effective election campaign strategy, you need to identify your “constituency” – the numerical groups united by common problems and hopes that you think will most easily convert into supporters of your candidate.

Then comes the challenge of expanding the candidate's social base to the extent necessary to win.

## To solve this problem, you need to find out:

1) intentions, goals, images of desired leaders for each of the target groups;

2) the political weight of each target group, i.e.its ability to influence the political situation, and then find approaches to them. The problems of the first direction are solved in the course of sociological surveys, political science and psychological, including psychosemantic, studies, when it is possible to confidently classify the population of the region of interest into groups, find out what problems voters in this group expect solutions to from the elections, their political preferences, the image of the “ideal leader”, who is the most preferred politician, electoral bloc, party, mayor, etc.

Political weight of the group

For the second direction - determining the political weight of each target group - there is its own methodology that uses a number of important factors for analysis, in particular:

- cultural and educational level;
- group size;
- income level;
- awareness;
- intragroup cohesion;
- political activity;
- the presence of an organizational structure and the ability to take organized actions.

Having received data on target groups, election campaign organizers need to decide: which segments of the population should their candidate rely on?

Practice shows that it is almost impossible to satisfy the requests of all groups; you should focus on a few, but so that their total weight ultimately exceeds 50% of voters.

First of all, you should pay attention to those groups to which the candidate is closest in terms of his social origin or position.

Secondly, we must try to attract nearby target groups to our side, since there are no continuous barriers between them, but there are smooth transitions, often coinciding interests on some issues.

Let us make a reservation that by the words “reliance on this or that group” we mean not so much open appeals as unobtrusive rapprochement through meetings, explanatory work, and confidential negotiations with the leaders of these groups. If the candidate directly states: “My voters are entrepreneurs, all hope and support lies in them,” then this can lead him to complete isolation from other layers, and ultimately to a crushing defeat. Therefore, while always keeping in mind the main allies, we must not forget that many problems are close to all groups of voters, and the program must put forward universal goals for all target groups.


Key issues

The key problem of a particular target group is, to a certain extent, its Achilles heel, something that can quickly and emotionally influence its beliefs about you. Therefore, the team’s specialists, long before the elections, begin to identify in each address group a hierarchy of problems that are significant to it. Technologies that allow them to be identified were identified



above, and therefore you just have to correctly set a task for the team’s researchers and expect specific results.

If you find issues that you think most voters care about, double-check before you make them the focus of your campaign.

It is extremely important to identify the key issue or set of issues around which the campaign should revolve. This significantly helps to focus the chosen strategy on it.

Analysis of previous elections

This is the so-called history of election campaigns in the district. Use past voting results to rank polling stations by their voting behavior and how many voters actually turn out to vote. By analyzing the behavior of voters in the past, it is possible to determine the strengths and weaknesses of the current campaign and identify additional opportunities. Analyzing this data in conjunction with the rest of the material will help you accurately predict voter behavior on Election Day. To do this, it is necessary to collect the following materials: 1) the number of voters in the district and specifically in each of its precincts;2) what was the voter turnout at polling stations and, if possible, distributed by time of day (interval - half an hour);3) political preferences of the electorate: who is “loved” and who is “disliked” in each polling station. It would be very good to connect existing preferences with their reasons and socio-economic roots. A connection with political history, demography, social composition, crime rate and the rest of the list of issues that interest you is necessary;4) the number (as a percentage) of spoiled ballots;5) recorded incidents (“elective” hooliganism);6) other useful information.

## Possible sources of such information:

local election commissions at various levels; local executive and representative authorities (those who organized these elections);

local and regional political and social structures; those who observed the elections;

all kinds of analysts and lovers of collecting necessary and unnecessary information.

Determining the weight of the media

In order to rationally distribute the resources and forces of the campaign, it is necessary to determine the weight of each of the regional media.

1. Total weight: the cost/audience ratio, in other words, how many potential voters the information will reach and at what cost.

2. Program weight. To implement a set of various programs (for example, “Pensioners”, “Students”, “Enterprises X”) it is necessary to find out the media preferred by voters, their significance and authority in this category.

Interview with the candidate

Purpose of this interview


clarify the candidate’s views on certain positions, as well as force him to take a more detailed and sober look at his motives, intentions and chances of winning. This interview will allow you to determine the direction in which you will develop the candidate's image, plan an election campaign strategy, and outline further actions.

As a rule, the interview is conducted at the zero stage of the election campaign in the presence of the entire “kitchen office” and regardless of how well you know the candidate.



Listen carefully to the candidate and note the turns of his speech: you can then use them in preparing the candidate for speeches and contacts with voters.

In addition, the purpose of such an interview is to determine the framework of cooperation, distribute powers and responsibility for the course of the campaign. This will allow you to avoid many misunderstandings and conflicts in the future.
