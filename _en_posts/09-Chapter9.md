### Meetings with voters


No matter how hard your candidate tries to avoid meetings, he will still meet with his voters somewhere.

Voter targeting

Targeting voters allows you to determine WHO and WHERE your election campaign should primarily reach. You will get the maximum effect if you correctly select the most important and significant areas and allocate resources and efforts accordingly.

its own

Even in the same district, the situation is different at every new election. Sometimes it is very difficult to develop the only correct voter outreach strategy that would be most effective for your campaign.

Ultimately, the wisdom of your strategy will be decided on Election Day. But one thing is clear: the better you know your voter and the more consistent and planned your actions are, the more chances you have to win.

It is also important to constantly remember the task of the election campaign is not only to win supporters, but also to ensure their maximum turnout at the polling station. Constantly ask for the support of your supporters, present the situation in such a way that without their active participation, victory will be impossible.

Contact Program Goals

During the election campaign, first of all, it is necessary to influence those who will definitely take part in the vote. It is clear that convincing a person who has a negative attitude towards participating in elections in general is a waste of time and effort.

The second priority direction was determined in his sympathies.

## And the third goal is to stimulate supporters and ensure their turnout at polling stations on election day. Contact programs promote:

- recognition of the candidate and strengthening his position, establishing feedback,

strengthening confidence in the candidate and his campaign, identifying and attracting volunteer assistants. In addition, in the course of ascertaining the views of voters, the candidate has the opportunity to gain a true understanding of how his image and program are perceived, and to find out what expectations voters have of him.

Contact types

## There are many different forms of voter contact, and often only a combination of these tactics can be most effective. Voter contacts include:

specially organized events: rallies, scheduled meetings with voters (organized by precinct election commissions);

meetings at the place of work: meetings of labor collectives (at enterprises), parent meetings (schools, kindergartens); meetings at places of interest: in clubs, sections, circles; meetings at the place of residence: the “every door” campaign, meetings in the yard and near the house, visits to hospitals, nursing homes, blitz meetings with voters (near shops, at public transport stops);

- appearances in the press, on TV and radio (“messages” on current topics, presentation of one’s program, participation in debates with competitors);

- telephone calls to voters;

meetings with volunteers and “opinion leaders”;

participation and organization of special events (festivals, sports competitions, awards, fireworks, fairs, concerts, distribution of garden plots); speeches at conferences, meetings, seminars of political, public and professional organizations; the candidate’s personal participation in high-profile “good deeds” (opening or completing the construction of a hospital, actions in extreme situations, for example, an environmental accident).



Types of Leaflets

Depending on the stage and specific tasks of the election campaign, the most typical leaflets and appeals to voters can be identified. These tasks are decisive.

The “Meet the Candidate” leaflet aims to introduce the candidate’s personality, views and program. It is often made in the form of a double postcard and is used in the first stages of election campaign. Booklet “My Program”

materials that reveal the views and image of the candidate, raise problems and proposed ways to resolve them, answering the question: “Why am I seeking your trust.”

- Leaflet-letter - a more personalized and personal appeal with a request to support the candidate, provide certain services (put up posters, organize a meeting, etc.).- Congratulation leaflets are used on the eve of major holidays (for example, March 8) to create a positive IMAGE of the candidate.

- Leaflet (booklet) “We know him” with the support of authoritative, popular people in the district calling for voting for the candidate. Sent out on the eve of the elections.

- Leaflet “They will not pass” (or “We will win”) Purpose: polarization of the positions of the candidate and his main opponents, differentiation from competitors and an indication of the advantages and uniqueness of the candidate’s program, comparative analysis of the programs of rivals (“Why am I better”).

- “Attacking leaflets” - containing information about the negative aspects of the biography or the unpopular position of the opponent. Such materials have a good effect on opponent’s supporters, shaking their unshakable confidence in their chosen one.

Planning your contact program


most

Always remember, a personal meeting between a candidate and voters is a stronger and more effective move in the entire election campaign. This is the best way to purchase additional votes.

Try to take into account all the details of the program: what needs to be purchased to hold the event and prepare the necessary materials, who needs to be paid for what, how many participants and volunteers need to be involved, who needs to be invited.

The best plans have to be rejected if they cost too much. Try not to get into this situation. Timely estimate the cost of the program you are going to implement. At the same time, don't let money completely dictate your campaign strategy. If you don't have enough money, take it from other programs.

First of all, you must concentrate your resources and direct them to the areas that are most important to you. You need to start with them and end with less important areas.

Who will lead the program?

A voter engagement program requires careful management. Therefore, do not start planning without identifying the person who will manage the program. Most often, the program coordinator is a specific person who works closely with the district director and territory director. He is expected to have the ability to communicate effectively, have good organizational skills, and be collected.

Attracting volunteers

All voter outreach programs are typically based on the active participation of volunteers. The fewer financial resources you have, the more volunteers you need to have. Therefore, if your primary focus is on contact programs, the importance of the director of volunteer relations and the scope of his work increases. You must develop and implement a system for searching and attracting volunteers, forming them into an efficient team and preparing them for work.

It’s good if voters see many of their friends and neighbors on the candidate’s side. A person is inclined to trust the opinions of people he knows and respects. Therefore, personal support from people known and respected in the area can have a positive impact on the results of the contact program.

It is highly desirable that the candidate be accompanied at such meetings by a well-known person who enjoys authority and respect in the area. This will also provide support for the candidate and pave the way to victory.

“Every door” program

The “every door” campaign is based on the “neighborhood principle”. The candidate comes to voters like a neighbor, explains the purpose of his



visit, why he decided to run and why he needs their support. Such personal contact is good for attracting supporters and finding volunteers. And pre-compiled conversation scripts and candidate preparation will help carry out the program without any problems.

This form of communication with voters is popular (and, perhaps, the only one available) in small towns and rural areas, as well as when conducting ECs to local (district, city) bodies

authorities.

## Remember that the main task of a contact is to make a favorable impression. The appearance of a candidate in a voter's apartment is a pleasant surprise. True, this form of communication requires listening to complaints and wishes. Lots of time and patience Ask questions about voters' concerns and needs. However, try not to get bogged down in the conversation, but to interrupt contact in a timely manner:

“I was very glad to meet you and talk with you. Be sure to come to the polling station on March 29.My last name (first name), I am running for the City Council. I really need your support. Thank you. Goodbye".

Contact requirements

## Contact requirements are the same as in the case of agitators (see “Recommendations for agitators”). An example text for entering into contact could be like this:

“Good afternoon, I am (name and surname), your neighbor, a candidate for the city council. I want to be elected because (main motive). Can I count on your support on Election Day, March 29?”

When you say your name, shake the other person's hand and look straight into their eyes, smiling naturally and kindly. All your behavior should indicate confidence, reliability and sincerity. There is no need to enter the apartments for a long time. Short meetings are important - a greeting, a few words, a smile, farewell.

Among the employees accompanying the candidate, it is advisable to have those who know the area well and can help in collecting information, or those who, with their authority, will give weight to the candidate.

It is especially effective to carry out the “every door” program in high-rise buildings, “dormitory” areas and dormitories. A large concentration of voters greatly facilitates work in winter and saves time.

Political rallies

It may seem that the time for violent rallies is over. However, they are always effective if well organised, especially in local elections where there is a need to energize supporters, inspire volunteers and attract media attention.

## If the meeting is being organized by the election commission and you have to speak with your opponents, it is important to remember the following:

the main task of your speech


stand out from the crowd. You need to find your own unique form of speaking and talk about anything but what your opponents are saying. It's not so important what you talk about at these meetings, it's HOW;

try to attract and retain attention using special techniques (see “Preparing the Candidate”). You should choose an aggressive, offensive, ironic manner, speak convincingly and brightly. The use of metaphors and specific examples in speech has a great effect; Try not to get defensive, get into debates, or get lost in ideas. The more verbose you are, the more vulnerable you are to attack and criticism. As a rule, the participants in such Meetings are “politically concerned” voters (they are also often opinion leaders in their circle) and support groups for your opponents. Therefore, be prepared for any provocative and unpleasant questions, as well as “unexpected” reactions from listeners.

Meetings at your place of residence


very attentive

It's easiest to find older people at home. And this is the personal audience during contacts with them as voters. In addition, this category of people is the best “relay” of information and, as a rule, enjoys the necessary authority. One of the types of work in “dormitory” areas is meetings in the yard. As a rule, they are organized simultaneously with yard gatherings and meetings of residents. At such meetings, one of the local topics is seriously discussed, most often communal - transport, water, roads, building repairs, garbage collection, improvement of the area, infrastructure development.

Organizational scheme for such a meeting: with the help of volunteers, create an initiative group, determine the topic of the meeting and establish



time. Select a meeting topic that is specific and significant for the majority of voters in this house for discussion (for example, when will the hot water be turned on, the elevator open, the kindergarten completed, etc.). It is useful to invite a responsible official (from the housing office, district council) to this meeting, who can give competent explanations on all issues. Place flyers and posters announcing the meeting at entrances in advance. Half an hour or an hour before the meeting, volunteers go through the apartments and invite those wishing to take part in a meeting with the candidate. You can leave (as a reminder) a special invitation card with the topic of the meeting, the name of the candidate and the campaign slogan.

The candidate's main task at this meeting is to present his program, listen to complaints, answer questions, and demonstrate his ability to solve problems. The duration of such a program is 1.5-2 hours. The effect is very significant. The meeting participants will spread this news to all residents of their own and nearby houses.

Blitz meetings with voters

Blitz meetings allow you to actively involve a large number of volunteers and reach a huge number of people, not only residents of certain houses and areas, but also just people on the streets of the city. For blitz meetings

places of spontaneous gathering of people are used: markets, train stations, transport stops. Well organized blitz meetings


this is great

a channel for disseminating information about your candidate at minimal cost and in a short period of time. Blitz meetings are especially effective at the first stage, when you are the first to start (remember, your goal is to stand out from the crowd).

It is very effective to accompany blitz meetings with large-scale outdoor propaganda: posting posters and leaflets, banners, flags, shields, inflatable balloons, as well as various “noise effects” when a megaphone and music are used.

Speeches before work groups

This is an effective way to meet a large group of voters with a common interest, with minimal expenditure of resources.

## Meetings at enterprises have a number of significant advantages:

there is an opportunity to meet with a group of voters who are united by a common cause, interests and common problems; attendance is ensured, issues with the meeting room are resolved;

the audience is quite favorable;

- listeners will gather in the right place at a certain time and will certainly sit until the end of the performance. The only limitation of this form of contact is that the candidate (or members of his team) must have good personal relationships with the leaders of the organization.

A visit to a plant or factory requires a well-thought-out plan. The first step is to determine the number of employees in the company and how many of them are your voters. Depending on the importance of the undertaking, you will be able to assess the feasibility of the effort that needs to be expended to organize the performance.

Preliminary information

## You should get complete information:

about the specifics of the enterprise, its products and history; what problems this enterprise faces and what ways are there to solve them;

what problems its employees face (as a rule, employees of large enterprises live in the same area and have common social problems).



It should be understood that in large enterprises there are two camps - managers and employees. When you plan your visit, don't forget that they have different problems and interests. Therefore, it will be better to meet with both at different times.

Your meeting will go better if people are prepared for it. To do this, you will need one or more assistants who work at this enterprise, can accompany you, know the balance of power and will help you avoid annoying mistakes.

Meetings in institutions

The same principles form the basis of business visits (to offices, institutions, shops) and visits to health care institutions, etc. Such meetings can have two purposes: contacts with voters and conducting a kind of inspection. Both hospital patients and those who work there usually respond with gratitude for the attention they receive and are willing to vote in support. Visits may be accompanied by actions of “real assistance” to a specific institution (for example, the supply of necessary equipment, medicines). It is more advisable to do this at the “finish line”, actively involving the press and television.

"Spontaneous" meetings

If you have not agreed with management about organizing a meeting at the enterprise, you can organize a “spontaneous” meeting near the entrance. When you organize such a meeting, consider the following. The main thing for an employee is to get home as soon as possible and come to work on time. Therefore, be very careful and moderate. It is best to develop special events (for example, handing out materials, selling products at wholesale prices) that will not irritate or anger people. Work with people who go to work, not people who go from work.

Holidays, concerts

All the most important incidents take place on the main streets of the city. The candidate can use them for meetings with voters (exhibitions, fairs, citywide holidays). Try to have the candidate appear at these meetings in the company of people known and respected in your district.

As experience shows, meetings are better held in crowded places. During holidays, folk festivals, fairs, this creates a positive background and an appropriate mood.


MEETINGS WITH VOTERS

Such events have great resonance both from a political point of view and in terms of press coverage. The candidate has the opportunity to demonstrate himself and at the same time “hook” on some important problems.

It is important to have several volunteers with you to hand out literature and draw people's attention to the candidate. The “announcer with a megaphone” is often practiced, the main goal of which is to invite people and attract attention (in order not to tire passers-by, light music is played periodically, the main thing is to create a festive atmosphere). Don't forget about outdoor campaigning as well. The only thing the candidate should do in this case is meet people, exchange remarks, smiles and handshakes.


The main advantage of such campaigns is the positive reaction of the press. The press loves to comment on such events because reporters see the candidate surrounded by people. When skillfully organized, this approach demonstrates the candidate's advantages.

By the same principle, meetings should be organized in dormitories (students', factories), educational institutions, etc. Often, candidates organize loud competitions (for example, with the distribution of beer), lotteries, performances by popular groups, discos. However, experience shows that very strong motivation is needed for young people to come to the polls.

Summing up

## The day after the event, you should look back and analyze what you managed to achieve. Be sure to evaluate the preparation of the event, the actions of key figures and the candidate, and the reaction of voters. Try to answer the questions:

What needs to be done to make future events even more successful?

- Have the strategy and tactics of contacts with voters been chosen correctly?

What tasks do you set and which ones do you actually solve? Be sure to thank everyone who attended the event.
