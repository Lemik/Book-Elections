## CHAPTER TEN

### Election Day

In principle, you can do nothing on this day, considering that a lot of propaganda work has already been done. But there is nothing you can do during campaigning and a different approach and ensure your victory on this very day.


Ensuring attendance

Election Day is the finale for which you and your staff have been preparing throughout the campaign. On election day, all efforts come to an end. You can successfully implement a number of programs, but if you do not spend this day at a high level, you may lose everything.

As practice shows, no more than a quarter of all voters vote on their own initiative. The rest need special motivation, an additional push to take part in the vote. In addition, all former Soviet people are accustomed to receiving persistent invitations to go to the polls on election day. All your work may be in vain if your candidate's supporters don't show up to cast their votes on Election Day. Therefore, on election day it is necessary to attract maximum forces to stimulate voters, since election commissions will no longer do this.

Search for supporters

Preparations for Election Day begin long before it arrives. Every day, campaigners and volunteers record information about clear supporters of the candidate. This information, if possible, should include: address, telephone number, full name, number of family members.

On this day, don’t forget about the volunteers and their vibrant energy.

Monitoring the progress of elections

Of course, the voting control program is aimed not only at preventing fraud, but also at avoiding mistakes that are made by coincidence.

Disturbances, accidental or special, can cause significant changes to the final tallies. Many campaigns were lost due to the loss of a few votes in each precinct. Such a program can only be successfully implemented if there is knowledge of the laws and the availability of personnel who can implement the program.

Election observers

The voting and counting monitoring program requires the presence of at least one well-trained election observer at each precinct.

This requires special personnel who must immediately report all violations to the relevant authorities and the media. Experience shows that the mere fact of the physical presence of observers is more effective than any combination of research into election legislation and public relations.

Find out what credentials and documents confirming them are required for observers. Work this out with your legal team in a timely manner.

Polling stations

## Arrange all sections in the following order:

areas that were previously suspected of violating the rules; areas where voting takes place with a large number of voters and a very sharp fight is expected; areas where your candidate is expected to lose; areas where your candidate is expected to win.

The most experienced supervisors and people you can count on should be assigned to the most important areas.

Actions on Election Day

Your effectiveness on Election Day depends on your specific campaign resources. A special operations center must be created by Election Day. Moreover, it is better to place it directly near the headquarters of your election campaign no later than the day before the elections. The election control campaign manager and legal counsel should be on hand to guide others and make appropriate decisions when necessary. Volunteers, transport, etc. should also be on hand.

Incident Reports

There are two types of reports: an incident report and an election observer report.

An incident report is a short form used to state a complaint or formulate



problem. Such messages can come from various sources: from party functionaries, voters or observers.

Reporting an incident requires an immediate response, either by sending a special legal team to the site or by visiting the site with the head of the election monitoring team. Copies of incident reporting forms must be distributed to the voting control system, i.e., to all observers. This allows you to control and distribute by importance the flow of complaints that arrive at the operations center.

## If the observer detects any violations, he must indicate the following in his message:

the time when the incident took place;

the name of the voter or official involved in the incident;

description of the incident and measures taken;

the names of people who can corroborate his report;

certificates certifying the accuracy of the report (signed, if possible).

An election observer's report is a more formal protocol-type document: a description of events that occurred while the observer was on duty. A uniform procedure for submitting such reports should be established.

All observers must have a clear understanding of their responsibilities on election day. The main task of the observer is to monitor compliance with the voting procedure and record its violations, promptly responding to them in the manner prescribed by law. An observer must report to his/her polling station at 7:30 a.m., half an hour before opening, to be able to observe the preparation of the polling station for voting, introduce himself to the members of the election commission, and receive the document necessary to work as an observer.

## The observer must have with him: an observer ID, a passport, voting instructions, pen and paper, a watch, a camera, a flashlight, food and water, a calculator, as well as a list of “hot” telephone numbers and clear instructions on what to do in the event of a violation of the voting process. Main types of violations

Throwing in falsified (pre-prepared) ballots

The most common type of fraud in the past. It was carried out, as a rule, by election commission workers on command from above, and later for other reasons. The only way to prevent this is the conscientious and coordinated work of observers. Illegal assistance

tit it



This is one of the typical cases of voter fraud where an official accompanies the voter into the booth as if to provide assistance.

Bribes or threats

Sometimes voters are offered a bribe to vote or are threatened with deprivation of any benefits and some forms of services at their place of residence (repairs, delivery of pensions, etc.) if they do not vote “as needed.”

Illegal vote

In every election, hundreds of votes are cast by those who are not eligible to vote. Make sure that people who do not live in the area do not vote.

Duplicate voting

Sometimes voters, like “ghosts,” walk from polling station to polling station, voting under the names of people who have already died or moved, or who did not come to the polling station.

Distracting the attention of election observers

Observers may be involved in conflict situations, disputes, and then they will be reported as the initiators of a scandal that caused some unwanted problems. They may also be distracted by fake phone calls.

Digital fraud

Poll workers may change or rearrange numbers in columns when counting votes. Issuing multiple ballots

Sometimes a voter receives multiple ballots and votes for their candidate multiple times.

Disqualification of voting

The instructions for filling out ballots are always very precise, but an unscrupulous official may tear or mark a ballot in some way and then disqualify it.

Candidate's activities

It is better for a candidate to be in public on election day. It is rare that a candidate is at campaign headquarters or at home, e.g.away from the main events. The candidate must start the day by voting himself, and do so as early as possible. The candidate must work on Election Day under a very strict schedule that includes visits to polling stations, phone calls to influential supporters, participation in blitz meetings with voters and other public appearances.

The candidate must be prepared to make a statement to the media about victory or defeat.

Celebrating victory

Plan a celebration if you win. Assign volunteers to prepare the celebration. Depending on your desire, it can be a modest dinner or a grand reception. Above all, it will be a celebration for your volunteers and supporters.



Reserve a place for your celebration. Sometimes they celebrate a victory right at headquarters. But the business of the day must be completed first, and the celebration should not be mixed with efforts to uncover voter fraud or voter final calls.

Summing up

Summing up and analyzing the results is a necessary stage, which is the natural conclusion of the election campaign. As a rule, it is not carried out immediately after the vote, but after a short time to stabilize the emotional (both positive and negative) result of the elections. On the other hand, this stage cannot be delayed, since such things as the “natural disintegration” of the team, new problems, normal turnover, and simply erasing individual details from memory occur.




When summing up the results, the work of the entire team and each of its members is assessed and impartially analyzed, what results were achieved, at what cost, the main mistakes and errors, what was the influence of uncontrollable factors on the campaign and in general, how close the implementation of plans and strategy turned out to be as planned, and much more.

Summing up is necessary not at all in order to reward someone or scold someone, but to evaluate the most important thing taken away from the campaign and the experience of its implementation.


For real serious politicians, election campaigns continue throughout their political life.
