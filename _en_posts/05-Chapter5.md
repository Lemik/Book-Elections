Information contexts


The following types of information contexts are distinguished: - direct (blitz meetings with the candidate, election programs, leaflets, contacts with campaigners);


indirect (information from relatives and friends, public opinion leaders, media, rumors).

Types of political advertising

1. Advertising in the media and communications:

a) **indirect advertising** is, as a rule, free news information with a clearly defined informational reason:

- events of interest to the press;
- press conferences, press releases;
- mass events held with the participation of a candidate or party;
- party gatherings, anniversaries, holidays, competitions.

b) **direct advertising** - in **television**: paid campaigning in the press, on radio and **television**; feature articles and programs (for example, work with people with disabilities, speeches on current topics in the district, letters from voters, etc.);



- advertising TV and radio spots identifying the name of the candidate, developing the theme or position of the election campaign.

## 2. Outdoor advertising/visual campaigning - serves to strengthen the identification of the candidate’s name. There are:


posters with a photograph of the candidate and the main slogan of the election campaign; stickers, flags, shields and election campaign badges reflecting the image of the candidate and the main idea of ​​the election campaign;

inflatable balloons, mannequins;

inscriptions on walls, banners;

portable billboards with posters and basic information

about the candidate and his program.

## 3. Personal contact with voters is the most effective and reliable form of communication with voters:

meetings of the candidate with voters: on the streets, at enterprises, at home, etc.;

the work of agitators “at every door”;

mailing (or delivering) printed materials “to every mailbox.”

## 4. Distribution of information and propaganda materials:

leaflets;

booklets;

brochures, books, newspapers;

calendars, etc.(See “Meetings with voters”).

By spending money on press and TV, you are investing in reaching people who may not even live in your area. In addition, direct delivery has the main advantage of targeting and selection. You can selectively reach out to specific populations in your district.

name advertising

this advertising can help identify the candidate's name and create an effect of presence; advertising biography (image)

focuses on two or three main (program) issues, helps highlight the campaign theme and control debates with opponents;



- contrasting advertising - helps differentiate you from competitors, helps create a clear contrast on a number of pressing issues, presents the candidate in a favorable light against the background of his opponents, allows you to name specific reasons why a voter should vote for your candidate;


supporting materials demonstrate support for the candidate from the “third force”: famous public figures, local leaders, and people with a high popularity rating.

Main theme of election campaign

An extremely important element of any election campaign strategy is its central theme.

A theme is a key message that is constantly conveyed during an election campaign to persuade voters to vote for your candidate and against his opponents. This is a simple statement that conveys the essence of the candidate's personality and position.

The topic must be present in all election materials. The topic is born in the specific political situation in the district and depends on the strengths of your candidate and the weaknesses of your opponents. It should arouse interest and trust.

During the brainstorming process, you may come up with many interesting and promising topics. Choose a campaign theme that will lead your candidate to victory. She

## should:

appear important to the candidate himself;

allow the candidate to best reveal himself to voters;

be directly related to the problems and needs of voters in a given district.

The main theme must be repeated constantly in the candidate's speeches and campaign materials in order to penetrate the consciousness



voters and influence their behavior. This is the most important information that needs to be maintained at all times, something that voters can connect with the candidate.

Modified theme


Different groups of voters may have their own modifications of the topic. The theme is not just a slogan, its purpose is to show voters that the candidate knows about their problems and is able to solve them in an optimal way.

The topic is formed based on the key issues of the district and should be sufficiently universal for different groups of voters. She addresses issues that concern voters. So the usual approach is to highlight a shared issue among voters and talk about your candidate's unique ability to address it.

Each point of the program must necessarily contain two poles: 1) explain why voters should support your candidate;

2) imply and show why this candidate is a better choice than his rivals.

It is important for people to know why they should choose your candidate. You must give them reasons why they will trust him.

At the same time, one should always keep in mind the axiom: people will vote for a person if they believe that he understands their problems and only he is able to change something.

Preparation of the candidate program

Develop a program that will best reveal your topic during the election campaign. The draft program is made very long and comprehensive, and then it cuts off all the unnecessary stuff and reduces it to short and clear messages that reflect the main themes of the campaign. It is recommended to use the following scheme.

1. Describe the main problems existing in this region. It is advisable to obtain the necessary digital calculations when it comes to the economy, social sphere or crime. Rank these issues in descending order of importance. Select those where you believe the candidate has an advantage based on his or her competence and ability to solve those problems.



2. Identify the main measures aimed at solving these problems. The election program should not be turned into a catalog of feasible and impossible promises. Try to objectively evaluate certain measures and show ways and means of their implementation.

3. Who (or what) interfered with and prevented their decision from those “main enemies”.

name

4. Name your personal contribution to the implementation of these measures.5. Try to retell all the material in several ways available to you. Emphasize and clearly argue the most important points.

Entrust the writing of the program to professional journalists or advertisers.


Often, candidates use the provisions of the programs of their fellow technicians to “intercept slogans.”Perniks should not hesitate to take such a step if, in your opinion, it will benefit voters, and most importantly, it corresponds to the goals, objectives and priorities of your election campaign.

When creating a program, pay attention to strategic

## axes:

axis associated with

ideological attitudes of voters. Goal: to create and strengthen a sense of similarity in the political preferences of the candidate and voters. The most common technique is the polarization of opinions: left-right, conservatism - change, authoritarianism of freedom. Such opposition in programs should not be an end in itself; it is intended only to reinforce the demarcation of civil positions on certain problems;

social axis related to economic and social

voters' expectations. Goal: to draw attention to those problems that primarily concern voters and in which your candidate has obvious advantages;

axis related to the personality of the candidate. Goal: to strengthen the popularity or make your candidate well known in a short time, to introduce into the candidate’s image those traits that voters expect from him.

Main theme support

As your campaign progresses, each piece will add a new element to voters' understanding of your topic. By repeating the theme, in conjunction with other activities, voters can develop a clear understanding of the candidate and why they should choose him.



To create successful campaign materials, it is always important to clearly understand the purpose of these messages.

If you do a campaign long enough, it feels like you're doing the same thing over and over again. And no result. However, this is not true. The more often you repeat and elaborate on your campaign's central theme, and the more voters hear it, the more deeply and accurately the message penetrates their consciousness (the "information wave" principle).

In the end, this is what will have a decisive influence on the voting results. That is why the candidate’s appeals must be justified and repeated repeatedly. In speeches at rallies, in the press, on television, one and the same topic must be taken into account. A candidate's team often sends out campaign messages that are packed with too much stuff, as if it were necessary to present the candidate's comprehensive views on as wide a range of issues as possible in one leaflet.


However, the abundance of materials on various topics creates chaos in the minds of voters. As a result, at the end of the election campaign they do not have a clear idea of ​​what the candidate stands for and are not inclined to vote for him.

### Slogans

The main theme of the election campaign should be stated in simple terms. At the same time, you can have different messages to different target groups of voters, but they should all be tied to the same topic.

For example, for one of the businessmen, the image of an independent, strong business executive was chosen, with a whole program for liberalization and economic recovery, and the creation of new jobs. The slogan, aimed at a youth audience, was: “I will teach you how to make money.”

Working with the passive

It would be naive to assume that the majority of the electorate consumes long and serious materials on any political issue. He always has, even during elections, much more serious, useful, pleasant and meaningful things to do (for example, preparing for Christmas).

Understanding all this, as well as the fact that the struggle for the “passive” is in most cases the main battlefield, it is necessary to work with the media in such a way that the “passive” is very interested in consuming the materials produced or initiated by you.



One of the technologies for activating the “passive” involves presenting HIS problems to him in HIS language and at the EMOTIONAL level of perception of information.

Remember, we easily forgive offenses small and large, the manifestation of obvious shortcomings to those of our acquaintances and friends with whom we have established a warm, positive emotional connection (that is, those we like).

There is an opinion that the voter will vote for a competent person who thinks about the same problems as he does, wants to solve them and knows how to do it. And the only thing he lacks is to become that very deputy.

Communication problems

In the communication process, a number of problems may arise related to incorrectly chosen methods and means of disclosing the main topic of election campaign.

For example, it often happens that a candidate does not find the strength, sincerity and confidence to convince the electorate of something that he himself does not believe in.

The second problem is that every person (and the candidate is no exception), when communicating with another person or group of people, on an unconscious level believes that in front of him is the same as him, with the same thinking, worldview, life experience, scale of values ​​and views. In other words, by definition, he must understand the style of speech, its turns, words used, terms, links, examples, gestures, etc. from half a word.

Nevertheless, Carnegie also described why it is better to go fishing with worms as bait, and not with strawberries and cream, which dear Dale likes so much!

This is one reason why a candidate should have an effective communications expert on his team who can objectively and impartially evaluate his communication with voters in all forms. And he would not only evaluate, but also prepare the candidate, advise him and the press secretary.

### Information presence technologies

Information about a candidate, his team and his campaign in the media can be presented in several ways.

1. On equal terms with other candidates in print space or time frames for electronic media, which, as a rule, are free and agreed upon by the election commissions.



2. For a fee (to the media or journalist) in the form of explicit, hidden or indirect political advertising.

3. In news materials posted on the initiative of the editors (usually free of charge). It must be distinguished from being on the news for a fee. Moreover, in this case, the information does not necessarily have to be positive; very often the opposite happens.

4. In various kinds of appeals, statements, refutations, open letters and other elements of the political game.5. In editorial or analytical materials at the suggestion of the editors.

Information wave

Information wave, information cloud - these are just a few specific names of processes and technologies for presenting election information to the electorate. Not a simple submission, but one in which, with a minimum of costs, by the time of voting there is a maximum effect.

In fact, each team of image makers has its own “know-how” on creating and maintaining an information wave. But nevertheless, there are general approaches to technologies for effectively presenting information.

## Here are just a few of them:

information should not be evenly distributed throughout the election marathon. Depending on the time of the campaign, the level of the campaign, its resources, there can be from two to five to seven waves with peaks and valleys;

the beginning of an active presence is usually associated with an information “explosion” across all major media, followed by a relatively long period of first information “silence.”

A powerful start is necessary for further recognition during the next information peaks, and a temporary absence is necessary for assimilation and the emergence of immunity among the electorate from satiety and subsequent rejection; the time interval between peaks of information depends on a number of conditions: the time remaining before the elections, the characteristics of a given media, the region of information dissemination (its area, individuality, etc.). Some rightly consider this a kind of art on the verge of knowledge, experience and instinct;


peaks on different media may not coincide;


For better assimilation and memorization, it is advisable to activate only one position at each appearance. It is presented in a simple, colorful, short, easy-to-remember form; the activation of all other positions must be strategically coordinated and subject to strict internal logic, so that by the time of the election the voter has formed a positive, powerful and holistic perception of the candidate’s image

News materials

As a rule, information about a candidate is automatically included in news materials in a limited number of cases: at the time of registration, in case of victory, if something out of the ordinary happens (from a wedding to a car accident).

## All other cases are in your hands, in the hands and head of your press secretary. But nevertheless, there are long-established rules for presenting information that claims to be news. Information (press release, press release, etc.) must be:

fresh (the media are persistently allergic to “rotten” news);- interesting for consumers of this media. There is a humorous criterion for interestingness: “Here is an ordinary man sitting at home, who doesn’t give a damn about anything, drinking tea and reading “with half an eye” (watching or listening with half an ear) our news. So, will he be distracted when this material appears, at least for a second or not?”;

emotional. In other words, is this topic significant for the region or not? Is it a topic for people to discuss at work, at home, or at leisure?

- short and beautifully written.

Preparation of materials

## When preparing material for the media, you must clearly understand the following:




the type and purpose of the specific material (candidate's program, interview, news material, press release, official statement, appeal, custom material, etc.); intended audience (language of the material, style, texture, etc.);

technical capabilities and distinctive features of the electorate’s perception of various media (features of TV, radio broadcasts and newspaper publications);




peculiarities of perception of various types of “presence” in the media (live broadcast, television or radio debates, interviews, etc.).

Preparation of information

If you want to get good press, prepare your information in a way that makes it easy for reporters to write about the candidate and his campaign. Don't rely on journalists to simply provide them with the facts and let them decide what is most important. Strive to control your own messaging and force the press to act in your best interests. How can this be done?




When offering information to the press, stay somewhere between "primitive" presentation of facts and outright self-promotion.

Frame your messages as news information that indirectly promotes election campaign interests. Create an informational occasion, use intrigue, plot resolution.

In each message, cover no more than three or four questions, united by one main idea.

Use keywords. State your main idea in a simple sentence that can be easily quoted. Repeat this key sentence in all materials sent to the press.

Don't overload your messages with too much information. Use every means available to promote your message: press release, phone interviews, radio appearances, etc.

Be consistent: all communications should support and promote the core theme of the election campaign and relate to its strategy.

Materials prepared for the media should be written simply and clearly. Materials are written to show how the issues raised affect people's lives.

Printed materials

## When preparing printed materials, you should adhere to some rules:

The election campaign sign should be simple and immediately evoke an association with the candidate. When designing it, ensure that the design emphasizes the message rather than clouding it. Use the sign in all items accompanying the election campaign; First of all, your materials should inspire trust and positive emotions. Before skimping on design



and printing, think about how this material will be used to judge the candidate and his election campaign;

posters should intrigue and capture attention. This is usually achieved by using an unusual image, a catchy word, a shocking phrase, varying the colors of print and paper, and using unusual sizes and shapes. The poster should be read very quickly (against your will) and “get stuck” in your memory;

- use attractive photographs of the candidate, avoid submitting “passport photos”;


use the “novelty factor” - an unusual design, colored paper, a non-standard envelope or an “irritating” phrase on the envelope will help arouse the voter’s interest; use family values ​​that “humanize” the candidate’s image: information and photos about family, children, pets, hobbies;

Remember that your constituent's attention span is limited. Don't try to say everything. And what you say should be simple and understandable. To increase the “life” of the leaflet, place on it (on the reverse side) some reference and information material (calendar of agricultural work, train schedule, list of “hot numbers”, etc.) or a list of candidates and a comparative analysis of their programs;

- establish a mandatory rule to proofread the material three times before submitting it to the printing house. Persemantic, second syntactic and third orthographic. It is advisable that control proofreading of even small materials be done by two or three people. Errors in leaflets cause irritation, which extends to the essence of the text and to the candidate himself. Pay special attention to writing phone numbers, dates, and last names.
